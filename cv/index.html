Research Experience

  • 2025 – Present

    Amherst, MA

    Research Assistant
    Laboratory for Advanced System Software, UMass Amherst
    • HyVox: Rethinking Low-Power Voice Intelligence towards Neuromorphic-von Neumann Edge Computing (under submission)
      • Built a hybrid neuromorphic–von Neumann system for always-on, open-set speaker identification: a neuromorphic chip embeds each audio window with sparse spiking, while an STM32 MCU scores speakers by cosine similarity, rejects unknowns, and enrolls new users and identifies them on-device without needing additional training.
      • Designed an 832-neuron spiking neural network (SNN) that learns a time constant per neuron across fast, medium, and slow timescales, plus a 32-neuron embedding extractor trained on enrollment episodes to generalize to unseen speakers.
      • Compressed the SNN 6.5x to fit the chip’s synapse budget, then built chip-accurate spiking training (CAST), which trains through a differentiable replica of the chip’s integer neurons and doubled top-1 accuracy on unseen speakers (31% → 61%).
      • Reached 99.6% top-1 and 5.5% rejection EER with 2 members (94.9% and 13.2% with 20) at 1.63 mJ dynamic energy per 3-s window, with 42% lower latency than the fastest embedded ANN and 5.8x less total energy than the most efficient ANN.
    • Neuromorphic On-Device Spoken Language Understanding
      • Building an end-to-end speech → phoneme → word → intent pipeline with no cloud speech recognition: the neuromorphic chip runs a feed-forward phoneme model, while the MCU holds context to assemble words and intents.
      • This division of labor lets just 400 spiking neurons reach 60% span accuracy over a 2,000-word vocabulary.
    • Energy-Adaptive Wearable Intelligence (UbiComp 2027)
      • Trained the baseline spiking models and built compute benchmarks for IMU-based activity recognition on SynSense Xylo, using FLOP profiling to map energy–accuracy trade-offs across operating points.
      • Helped develop a single runtime-adaptive model that scales its computation to the energy available, widening the operating range 10–40x with up to 3.3x fewer spikes and <1.5% accuracy loss.
